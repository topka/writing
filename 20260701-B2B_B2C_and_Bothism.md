# B2B, B2C, and Bothism

I was watching a webinar with a well respected marketer yesterday and during the Q&A he received a usual kind of question when marketing techniques are discussed: “does this work for B2B as well as for B2C?”

He answered with strong “YES!” followed by a phrase that got me thinking and led to this post: 
> “We're always trying to find differences. We're all the same!"

I think he was right—but only half right.

## “We're always trying to find differences.”
It resonates with me that the differences between B2B and B2C marketing are differences of degree, not kind. Yes, B2B has longer cycles, more stakeholders and higher price point. Organizations make purchases, but organizations don’t feel trust, recall brands or fear making the wrong decision. People do.  
A CFO evaluating a million-euro ERP implementation still wants proof, ROI and references—but they also want to feel confident they won’t regret the decision. That’s not B2B or B2C. That’s human. Brand building, emotional response and mental availability are as important in business context.
For us marketers it is easier to defend the budget with number of leads instead of brand perception. So we often exaggerate those differences until they become an excuse for ignoring marketing fundamentals and skipping the brand work. And then B2B-specializing agencies and ABM vendors like getting their share of the pie. Quite ironically, even these companies selling highly rational B2B solutions and idea still rely on brand, emotion and storytelling in their own marketing.

## “We're all the same!”
Every B2B marketer has seen technical proof, certifications or detailed product capabilities make the difference in a competitive deal. Especially when they’re tailored to the concerns of specific stakeholders like CFOs or IT leaders. Taking “we’re all the same” too literally can lead to ignoring B2B specifics and poses a risk of flattening marketing to just brand, mass-market promotion and emotional storytelling and as result, failing at achieving goals.
Even if we are conceptually similar as humans, in B2B, the marketer isn’t persuading one buyer. They’re often helping multiple stakeholders with different perspectives persuade one another in order to tip the table in the right direction.
It is also worth being critical here. Even when not wrong, someone saying “we’re all the same” may be simplifying for their own reasons. If they are teaching marketing fundamentals, for example, building a more complex picture for the audience is not beneficial.

## What then?
I think both generalization and polarization of B2B and B2C are mistakes. Generalization erases real B2B constraints (multi-stakeholder buying, longer cycles) and polarization erases the shared underlying principles (brand, mental availability).
My favorite marketing professor taught me one valuable concept — “Bothism”, to hold both ideas simultaneously and combine them in the right proportions ([link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/google-for-business-aunz_choosing-and-over-or-in-marketing-with-activity-7457949739767209984-BkR7/)). 
I’ve found myself returning to that idea of “bothism”: before labeling something as B2[?], and before framing myself as either of those, I first question which parts rely on universal human psychology, and which parts need to adapt to this buying context. It helps me choose what I will do.
