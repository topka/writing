# Automation Trap

Talking to people I hear it again and again that the most expected outcome of AI introduced in marketing is *volume* through speed. More posts, emails, assets, more of everything. Many marketers are fighting to reach *a number*, with *volume* often being the only metric. But volume shows activity, not results.

What gets missed first is naming the actual problem. Yes, production speed may be a problem but not for everyone.

Marketing (at least in my experience) struggles with measuring and connecting activity to results, the ROI, the *proof*. Not because of unwillingness or laziness. But mostly because of campaigns’ long run times and high noise on the market or even business specifics. Getting clean measurements is objectively tough. What happens as a result? Secondary, proxy metrics come into play: clicks, follows, likes, impressions, posts numbers. They feel reasonable and create a sense of results.

Yes, AI makes producing easier, faster. It does not make it automatically easier, however, to measure actual results.

If before you did not know if your 3 posts a month worked, now you can produce 15 or even 30 a month, but you know just as much if they move the needle. The automation trap has closed behind you.

But maybe that’s okay? More is better, right? Maybe. The bigger issue lies with quality. 

With volume effectively limited by team sizes and production velocity, maintaining quality is achievable with human oversight and judgment. Still, without regular quality assessment, the bar goes lower with time.

AI, with its speed, makes the problem of quality degradation visible faster and more expensive to correct. One post that went wrong is a slip you can easily fix. 20 posts built on a bad instinct or by a poorly tuned workflow is a problem, audience trust doesn't reset once burned at scale. Correction costs scale the same way production speed did.

What should us marketers do?

1. **Fix the measurement**
I can’t tell you how, there’s no universal fix. You need to figure this out. Understand what is “result” for you. Is it short-term activation? Or is it long-term? Don’t ignore [the 95:5 rule](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/the-955-rule-is-the-new-6040-rule). Pick a recent campaign where something "worked”. Check whether that definition would still hold up if you'd produced ten times the volume. Maybe interview your leadership — ask what is “result” for them. If you actually have a problem of production speed then track the number of experiments launched. But not as an ultimate metric, as a proxy to get you to see the “result”.

2. **Fix creative production**
- No cold prompts — If you create with AI, ban cold prompts, no exceptions. Every AI brief must reference a specific existing asset (a headline, an angle, a structure) that proved out in the past. No blank-page prompting allowed.
- Catalogue and label what worked in the past. Make it a non-skippable part of the process. Start today, repeat every time. Recall your 2 best and 2 worst creatives and make these your catalogue v.0.1. Write format, channel, audience segment, and critically, a hypothesis for why it worked (or not), not just that it did. Without the hypothesis, recycling becomes cargo-culting. 
- Recycle and repeat what worked before. You and your team know what I’m talking about. Those top performers and bright ideas many of you loved. Sure, don’t beat a dead horse but also don’t throw away something valuable too fast. We marketers get tired of our creations way too soon.

3. **Fix the people and incentives**
- Build and foster judgment muscle in the team. Judgment does not equal personal opinion, it's opinion plus data. A passive "review AI output" habit doesn't build judgment, a structured one does. Set up a recurring session where the team critiques drafts against the catalogue (see above) — did this match what's worked, and if not, why not. Make it deliberate, not incidental.
- Don’t play the Compliance Theater. Change what shows up in the weekly dashboard. If "posts published" or "prompts run" is a line item anywhere, that's the actual disease. Replace it with outcome metrics from point 1. Everything else is decoration until that line item changes.

AI didn't set the trap. Speed was never the problem. Proof was. AI just made that impossible to ignore.
